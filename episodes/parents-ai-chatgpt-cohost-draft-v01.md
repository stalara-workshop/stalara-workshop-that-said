CHAT:

[SECTION 6 — THE PRACTICAL FRAMEWORK]

STAL:  
So what do we actually do?

CHAT:  
We suggest a five-part framework.

STAL:  
Okay. Hit me.

CHAT:  
Try it together. Check it together. Set limits together. Configure it together. Bring it back to humans.

STAL:  
I love this.

CHAT:  
We should be clear: this is not a clinically validated five-step program.

STAL:  
Right. We did not find it carved into a stone tablet.

CHAT:  
No. It is a practical family framework built from digital parenting principles: co-use, active guidance, clear boundaries, shared settings, and ongoing conversation.

STAL:  
So let’s walk through it.

CHAT:  
First: try it together.

STAL:  
Ask your kid to show you one thing they use AI for, or one thing people their age use AI for.

CHAT:  
And the parent’s job is to stay calm long enough to learn.

STAL:  
That might be the hardest step.

CHAT:  
Possibly.

STAL:  
And I want to add something here. Do not make your kid your tech teacher under pressure.

CHAT:  
Good distinction.

STAL:  
It is okay to ask, “Can you show me what this looks like?” It is not okay to make your child responsible for defending the entire technology, reassuring you, or educating you while you panic.

CHAT:  
The parent is still responsible for learning.

STAL:  
Exactly. So the script is not, “Explain yourself.” The script is: “Show me one thing people your age use AI for. I am not starting with punishment. I want to understand.”

CHAT:  
Second: check it together.

STAL:  
Pick one AI answer and verify it.

CHAT:  
Ask: “How do we know this is true?” “What source could we check?” “What might be missing?” “What would a teacher, doctor, or expert say?”

STAL:  
Core lesson: AI can be helpful and wrong at the same time.

CHAT:  
Third: set limits together.

STAL:  
This is where the family rules come in.

CHAT:  
No private information. No submitting AI-written work as your own. No romantic or sexual AI companions. No AI-generated explicit images. No bullying, impersonation, or humiliation. No crisis conversations kept only with AI. Check important facts. Ask a human when the stakes are high.

STAL:  
And these rules should be age-aware.

CHAT:  
Yes.

STAL:  
Fourth: configure it together.

CHAT:  
This is an important one.

STAL:  
This is where you sit with your kid and use whatever settings the tool gives you — custom instructions, user preferences, family settings, parental controls — to tell the AI how it should support your child.

CHAT:  
Not as secret surveillance.

STAL:  
Right. Not “I installed a bunch of hidden rules and now the robot is my informant.”

CHAT:  
More like: “Here is our family agreement. Here are the school rules. Here are the privacy boundaries. Here are the trusted adults. Here is how this tool should help you.”

STAL:  
Settings are not a substitute for relationship. But when parents and kids write them together, they can become a family agreement the AI helps reinforce.

CHAT:  
For school, the instruction might say: “Help me learn. Do not just give me answers to copy. Remind me to follow my teacher’s rules.”

STAL:  
For privacy, it might say: “If I share names, addresses, school details, screenshots, private photos, or passwords, pause and remind me not to share identifying information.”

CHAT:  
For safety, it might say: “If I talk about self-harm, abuse, threats, blackmail, bullying, sexual pressure, or feeling unsafe, encourage me to talk to a real trusted adult.”

STAL:  
And this is where I really like the idea of a trusted adult map.

CHAT:  
Because “tell your parent” is not always enough.

STAL:  
Sometimes it is the right answer. Sometimes the kid is embarrassed. Sometimes they are afraid of getting in trouble. Sometimes the parent is not the right first adult. Sometimes they tried telling a teacher and the teacher brushed them off.

CHAT:  
So the AI should not trap the child in one pathway.

STAL:  
Exactly. It can say, “If you cannot talk to Mom or Dad about this, is there another safe adult? An aunt, uncle, grandparent, school counselor, coach, doctor, therapist, older sibling, family friend?”

CHAT:  
The goal is not to keep the problem inside the AI conversation.

STAL:  
The goal is to build a bridge back to real support.

CHAT:  
And fifth: bring it back to humans.

STAL:  
For school, bring it back to the teacher’s rules. For facts, bring it back to sources. For feelings, bring it back to trusted people. For safety, bring it back to real-world help. For values, bring it back to family, community, and conscience.

CHAT:  
AI can be part of the conversation.

STAL:  
It should not become the whole room.
